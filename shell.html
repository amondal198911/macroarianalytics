How to read the monitor

  • Blue means growth, a surplus or a healthy reading. Red means contraction, a deficit or strain. On the map and in tables, deeper colours are further from the middle.
  • Every figure carries its year. Official statistics take time to compile, so the latest year differs by country and indicator.
  • On country charts, the dashed grey line is the world average, so you can see whether an economy is doing better or worse than the world.
  • Annual figures come from the World Bank. Market prices are daily or monthly, so they are more recent.
  • Tap any economy’s name to open its profile, and pin the ones you follow so they stay one tap away.

About Macroeconomic Monitor and Forecasting

Macroeconomic Monitor and Forecasting is an independent website. It brings together official statistics on growth, prices, jobs, public finances and trade for more than 200 economies, along with daily and monthly market prices, so anyone can see how the world economy is doing and understand what the numbers mean.

Methods and notes

How the numbers on this site are gathered, calculated and checked, so you can judge how far to rely on them.

Where the numbers come from

Annual figures for every economy, region and income group come from the World Bank’s World Development Indicators. Market prices come from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (oil and gas), the World Bank’s commodity price data (gold), the Federal Reserve (the US 10-year yield and exchange rates) and the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (US consumer prices). For this release they were collected through DataHub’s open-data copies of those sources, then checked as described below.

How figures are calculated

Most figures are shown exactly as published. These are worked out on this site:

Current account, foreign direct investment and remittances, % of GDP
value in US dollars ÷ GDP in US dollars × 100, for the same year

Currency change against the US dollar
(last year’s average rate ÷ this year’s average rate − 1) × 100, with rates in local currency per dollar. A negative number means the currency weakened. For euro-area members, rates from before they joined are converted to euros at the official fixed conversion rates, so their series run smoothly. Any year in which a currency changes unit, such as a redenomination or a switch to the US dollar, is left blank.

US inflation, monthly
consumer price index this month ÷ the same month a year earlier − 1. October 2025 is blank because the source data has no index for that month.

GDP is shown in US dollars at current prices and population in millions. World and regional figures are the World Bank’s own aggregates. For growth, the World Bank weights economies by GDP at market exchange rates. The IMF’s headline world growth uses purchasing-power (PPP) weights instead, which give fast-growing emerging economies more weight, so its figure is usually higher.

Rounding, gaps and older figures

Percentages are stored to two decimal places and shown to one. Missing years are left blank, never estimated. There are two labelled exceptions: tables can show a figure up to two years older, marked with a small year such as ’24, and survey-based poverty and inequality figures use each economy’s latest survey from the previous five years on maps, rankings and Explore.

Colours

Maps and tables group values into seven bands chosen for each indicator. Blue means growth, a surplus or a healthy reading; red means contraction, a deficit or strain; for amounts such as GDP, darker means larger. The bands are shown in each map’s legend.

Security protection

To protect its content, this site records attempts to copy, save, print or inspect protected material: the time, the page, the browser type and screen size, and your account email if you are signed in. A visitor who repeats these attempts is paused for 10 minutes, and the site owner may be emailed about it. Nothing else is recorded, and the owner does not sell or share this information.

To remember your choices, the site keeps a few settings in your browser’s local storage: light or dark mode, pinned economies, the last economy you viewed, your comparison set, your chart range and log-scale choice, and whether the price ticker is paused. They stay on your device.
